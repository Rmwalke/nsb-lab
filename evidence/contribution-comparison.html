1. Contribution statement

For the explicit nonlinear periodic datum W=(sin z, sin x, sin y) at viscosity one, the manuscript supplies a computer-assisted certificate, uniform over smooth, real, mean-zero, divergence-free perturbations satisfying A₋₁(h)≤10⁻⁴, that the actual ordinary heat-seeded Picard sequence enters a common invariant set at its third iterate and converges on the whole time half-line in L²ₜA₀, with error at iteration n≥3 bounded by 6(9/10)ⁿ⁻³. Separate regularity results enlarge a specified previously stated cyclic-shear class.

This describes the manuscript’s claimed theorem and the difference established by this targeted comparison. The theorem’s validity remains dependent on its analytic proof and finite certificates. This audit verifies the comparison and elementary consequences; it does not independently prove the complete theorem. The class-inclusion argument was already present in manuscript §1.1: the new work here checks it against the accessible primary source rather than presenting it as a newly invented theorem.

2. Exact scope of the main theorem

On the normalized 2π-periodic torus, with no external forcing and viscosity one, define As(f)=Σk≠0|k|s|f̂(k)| and X=L²((0,∞);A₀). Theorem 2.1, printed page 5, assumes u₀=W+h, W=(sin z,sin x,sin y), and A₋₁(h)≤10⁻⁴. The perturbation remains smooth, real, mean-zero and divergence-free; it may have infinitely many Fourier modes.

v⁰=eᵗΔ(W+h);   vⁿ⁺¹=eᵗΔ(W+h)−Q(vⁿ,vⁿ)
‖vⁿ−u‖X ≤ 6(9/10)n−3, n≥3
∫₀∞ A₀(u(t))²dt < 9
‖uh−ug‖X ≤ 4757 A₋₁(h−g), when both h and g satisfy the radius.

The implementation burden in §§2.4–2.5 is a 17-block estimate retaining the full causal history, an invariant region centred on the unperturbed third iterate, and verified entry of the actual perturbed third iterate. The sequence is the prescribed ordinary global iteration, without an independently substituted seed or time restart. A rougher controlling norm does not make this a theorem for arbitrary nonsmooth distributions.

4. An exact example beyond the named elementary test

Take h=(1/5000)sin(2y)eₓ and u₀=W+h. This h is smooth, real, mean-zero and divergence-free. The two nonzero coefficients each have magnitude 1/10000 at wave numbers ±(0,2,0). Hence A₋₁(h)=1/10000 exactly and A₋₁(u₀)=3.0001. It satisfies the main theorem’s stated radius.

The complete heat seed satisfies A₀(eᵗΔu₀)=3e⁻ᵗ+(1/5000)e⁻⁴ᵗ, so its X-norm squared is exactly 4.500240005. Using the manuscript’s elementary bilinear constant c=1/(2√2), the origin-centred Banach sufficient test has

(4c‖eᵗΔu₀‖X)² = 9.00048001 > 1.

Therefore that specific global origin-centred norm estimate cannot certify this example, while the stated W-centred theorem does cover it. This does not establish divergence of the iteration or failure of every prior method. It also avoids comparing an ℝ³ Fourier-integral threshold to periodic Fourier coefficients.

For hm=(m/10000)sin(my)eₓ, integer m≥2, one has A₋₁(hm)=10⁻⁴ for every m, but A₂(hm)=m³/10000 and ‖hm‖Ḣˢ=ms+1/(10000√2). This is a uniform smooth family with no uniform higher-Sobolev bound when s>−1. These deductions expose the role of the chosen topology; they do not create new hypotheses.

5. Strict enlargement of one stated regularity class

Let P interchange x and y and Tf(x)=P f(Pᵀx). Then TU=W and T preserves the equation and every As norm. Since |k|≥1 on nonzero integer modes, A₋₁(Th)≤A₂(h). Keeping the same smooth, real, mean-zero and divergence-free perturbation conditions, the earlier cyclic class therefore maps into the manuscript’s Theorem 3.1 region C(8/7,1/100).

T{aU+h: 0≤a≤7/10, A₂(h)≤1/100}
⊊ {aW+h: |a|≤8/7, A₋₁(h)≤1/100}.

Strictness is independently checked in two ways. W lies in the latter set but its minimum A₂ distance from bW, 0≤b≤0.7, is 0.9, exceeding 0.01. At the same amplitude 0.7, take h=0.01sin(2y)eₓ: A₋₁(h)=0.005, whereas A₂(h)=0.04. Its separate Fourier support prevents another amplitude decomposition from removing that discrepancy.

This is a comparison for global PDE regularity. The main ordinary-Picard theorem is not extended to that larger class. No comparison is made between different action quantities, constants or cutoff geometries. The strict containment concerns the published explicit sets, not the maximum possible reach of either general method.
